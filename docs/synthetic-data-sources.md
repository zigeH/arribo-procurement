# Synthetic data v2: sources and assumptions

`evals/data/synthetic_mloptic.json` is generated by `internal/synth`. It is
**not MLOptic data**. It imitates the *shape* of what a custom precision-optics
maker buys, using public disclosures of MLOptic (茂莱光学, SSE 688502) and
public industry references. Every generator parameter is tagged `[S#]` (source
below) or `[A]` (assumption) in the code.

## Assumptions (`[A]`), to be replaced by real data

- Unit prices per category (except PCBA) and quantities per order.
- Reject rates (0.3–1.5% typical; one persona 6.5%). No public source found.
- Acknowledgment times; +12 h for overseas suppliers.
- Payment terms: domestic 月结30/60/90天, overseas Net 30; one 30% deposit case.
- How often a late supplier moves the promised date before it's due.
- Volume: ~730 POs over 18 months, ~25M CNY. Real purchasing is much larger
  (hundreds of millions of CNY a year per S1); this is a slice, not a model of scale.
- Fixed FX rates (scoring never compares across currencies).

## What realistic data changed in the scoring

1. **Price is only benchmarked against other suppliers.** Comparing a supplier
   with its own history always looks normal; the "overpriced" persona scored A
   until this was fixed.
2. **Lead time compares repeat orders only.** First orders legitimately take
   longer (S4).
3. **On-time is measured against the first confirmed date**, and moved dates
   are reported. Otherwise a supplier that keeps moving dates looks punctual.
4. Result on this data: **only 38% of lines can be price-benchmarked and 22%
   lead-time-benchmarked; 26 of 76 suppliers get no price score** because their
   parts are single-sourced. If MLOptic's real data looks like this, price
   benchmarking needs a different basis (by spec / material / size, or
   should-cost) for single-sourced custom parts. See the open issue.
